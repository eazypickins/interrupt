# Deep-Dive 01 — Gravity & Physics Breaks
## The Complete Sub-Technique Library (25 Prompt Variations)

> Part of the Scroll Interrupter Master System. This is the deepest and most
> versatile category — gravity is the brain's most primal expectation, so
> breaking it is the fastest possible scroll-stop.

---

## Why This Category Wins

The brain doesn't "think" about gravity — it *assumes* it, automatically, in the
first 50 milliseconds of processing any frame. When an object floats, falls up,
or a hole opens beneath someone, the brain hits a hard reality-violation error
and halts the scroll to resolve it. That halt is the entire game.

**Use this category when you need:** a guaranteed opener, an un-ignorable first
frame, or a "how is this happening" hook that works even on mute.

---

## The Physics Rules (Teach These to Your Students)

AI video models render gravity breaks best when you follow three rules:

1. **State the motion as physics, not vibes.** Write "the water flows upward
   into the glass," never "surreal water." Verbs + direction = renderable.
2. **Lock the camera.** The #1 failure is camera drift — the model "moves" the
   camera to fake the effect. Always write "locked camera, no zoom, pan, tilt,
   or rotation."
3. **Ground the scene in consistent lighting.** The illusion breaks if the
   floating object is lit differently from the room. Specify "lit consistently
   with the scene" every time.

## Sub-Technique Library

### 1.1 — THE FLOAT (Basic Levitation)

**Mechanism:** An object or person hovers with no visible support.

**Prompt 1 — Object Float:**
```
A single [object, e.g., a red apple] floats in mid-air, gently bobbing and
rotating slowly, with no visible support, against [a clean cream background].
A soft, realistic shadow is cast on the surface below, showing it is suspended.
Subtle weightless drift, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** strings, wires, visible hand, falling, resting on a surface.

**Prompt 2 — Person Float (Sitting Cross-Legged):**
```
A [person] floats cross-legged a few feet above [the ground/a bed], hovering
weightlessly with hair and loose clothing drifting as if underwater. Their
shadow is cast below on the ground. Serene, soft lighting, locked camera,
9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** standing, jumping, visible support, gravity, hair flat, static.

**Prompt 3 — Floating Above a Chair:**
```
A [person] sits in a meditation pose, floating several inches above [a chair/
cushion] that remains on the ground, empty. The person's fabric and hair drift
slowly, weightless. Consistent lighting between person and chair, locked camera,
9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** person touching chair, sitting on chair, no gap, static clothing.

**Prompt 4 — Multiple Floating Objects (Arrangement):**
```
A room where [multiple objects — books, a lamp, fruit, papers] float at
different heights, gently bobbing and rotating, as if gravity were switched off
only for the objects while the room stays normal. Warm cozy lighting, locked
camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** objects on surfaces, chaotic motion, no bobbing, static, camera move.

**Prompt 5 — Floating Food (CPG/Food Brands):**
```
A [food item, e.g., a glistening strawberry] floats and slowly rotates above
[water/milk/cream], droplets suspended around it, splash frozen mid-air.
Hyper-real, appetizing, high-speed camera aesthetic, locked camera, 9:16.
```
**Negative:** falling, splash in normal motion, blurry, dull, no droplets.

### 1.2 — THE FALL (Falling Through Frame)

**Mechanism:** A character or object enters from the top and falls out the bottom, limbs moving naturally.

**Prompt 6 — Character Falls (The Classic):**
```
A [character] enters from the top of the frame and falls straight down through
the center until they exit at the bottom. While falling, they move arms and
legs in continuous natural motion — arms bend, hands shift, legs softly kick.
Keep movement visible but subtle, fluid, realistic. Static background. Locked
camera, no zoom, pan, tilt, or rotation, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** frozen limbs, rigid pose, standing, walking, floating upright, floor, ground, portal, extra limbs, distorted anatomy.

**Prompt 7 — Object Falls in Slow Motion:**
```
A [fragile object, e.g., a ceramic mug] falls through the frame in slow motion,
tumbling end over end, with fine details and motion blur on the edges, against
[a clean background]. It exits the bottom without shattering (yet). Locked
camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** instant shatter, normal speed, no tumble, static fall, blurry subject.

**Prompt 8 — Falling Through Clouds:**
```
A [person] falls backward through [soft volumetric clouds / a bright sky],
limbs relaxed, hair and clothes streaming upward, an expression of [calm/
freedom/awe]. Dreamlike, soft light, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** panic, rigid body, no motion, clouds static, upward camera drift.

**Prompt 9 — Falling Into Water (Splash):**
```
A [person/object] falls from above and hits [water], creating a dramatic
splash with droplets frozen in ultra slow-motion, the moment of impact crisp
and detailed. High-speed camera aesthetic, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** normal-speed splash, blurry impact, no droplets, underwater shot, camera move.

### 1.3 — REVERSE GRAVITY (Things Fall Up)

**Mechanism:** Objects, liquids, or people rise instead of falling.

**Prompt 10 — Rising Rain:**
```
Reverse gravity: rain rises upward from [the ground / puddles] back into the
sky, droplets accelerating as they ascend, the world otherwise normal. A
[person] walks through the rising rain unaffected. Moody lighting, locked
camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** rain falling down, normal gravity, downward motion, no upward flow.

**Prompt 11 — Water Flowing Upward Into a Glass:**
```
Reverse gravity: a stream of water flows upward out of [a pitcher / a tap] and
into an upside-down glass above it, defying gravity smoothly and continuously.
The water behaves realistically — surface tension, droplets — only the direction
is reversed. Clean studio lighting, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** water falling down, normal pour, splashing downward, no upward flow.

**Prompt 12 — Hair Defying Gravity:**
```
Reverse gravity: a [person] stands normally, but their hair floats straight
upward as if gravity were inverted only for them, moving gently like underwater.
Fabric also drifts upward slightly. Warm lighting, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** hair flat, hair falling normally, no upward motion, static hair.

**Prompt 13 — Floating Debris Rising:**
```
Reverse gravity: scattered [objects — papers, leaves, pebbles] rise from the
ground into the air, accelerating upward, as if the world's gravity has flipped
for them alone. Continuous, smooth, physically-lit. Locked camera, 9:16.
```
**Negative:** objects falling, objects on ground, no motion, downward drift.

### 1.4 — ZERO-G / WEIGHTLESSNESS

**Mechanism:** Everything in the frame floats, like in space.

**Prompt 14 — Zero-G Room:**
```
Zero-gravity room: [a person and every object in the room — books, a lamp,
a mug, papers] float weightlessly, drifting slowly and colliding softly,
hair and fabric suspended. Dust motes float in light shafts. Cozy warm light,
locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** objects on surfaces, gravity, falling, grounded, normal physics.

**Prompt 15 — Zero-G Kitchen (Food/Cooking):**
```
Zero-gravity kitchen: [ingredients and utensils — an egg, flour, a whisk, a
bowl] float and tumble weightlessly around a [person] who reaches out and
catches them mid-air. Flour dust drifts in suspension. Warm appetizing light,
locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** ingredients resting, falling, no suspension, gravity, messy splatter.

**Prompt 16 — Astronaut-Style Drift (Person):**
```
A [person] drifts weightlessly through a [domestic room / office], arms and
legs floating loosely, hair suspended, as if in zero gravity, slowly rotating.
Furniture stays grounded while the person floats. Soft light, locked camera,
9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** walking, standing, gravity, hair flat, stiff body, no drift.

### 1.5 — THE GROUND OPENS (Falling Into a Hole / Portal)

**Mechanism:** The floor opens beneath the subject, who falls backward into it.

**Prompt 17 — The Classic Ground-Hole Fall:**
```
Frame stays static while a character takes a step backward as a [dark hole/
portal] opens in the ground behind them and they fall backward into it, feet
last. Camera position stays fixed, no zoom or pan. The hole's edges glow
faintly. 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** character walking forward, staying on ground, hole closing, camera movement, jumping, levitating.

**Prompt 18 — The Sinkhole (Ground Collapses):**
```
A [person standing on the ground] suddenly drops as the [ground / floor]
collapses beneath them into a sinkhole, dust and debris rising, the person
falling backward with arms raised. The camera stays locked. Gritty realistic
lighting, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** person jumping, ground intact, no collapse, camera shake, no dust.

**Prompt 19 — Quicksand / Swallowing Floor:**
```
A [person] slowly sinks into [the ground / a floor] as if it were quicksand,
the surface rippling and swallowing them, panic turning to acceptance, hands
reaching up last. Surreal but physically lit. Locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** person on surface, no sinking, sudden drop, hard cut, no ripples.

### 1.6 — BOUNCE & IMPACT EXAGGERATION

**Mechanism:** Gravity works, but with impossible physics — exaggerated bounce, squash-and-stretch, or delayed impact.

**Prompt 20 — Rubber Ball Person:**
```
A [person] bounces like a rubber ball — each landing squashes their body
slightly and each leap stretches it, with exaggerated cartoon physics, bouncing
[down a street / through a room]. Smooth, bouncy, comedic, locked camera,
9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** rigid body, no squash and stretch, realistic gravity, stiff landing.

**Prompt 21 — Delayed Gravity (Cartoon Hang):**
```
A [person/character] runs off [a cliff / a ledge] and, for a long beat, hangs
suspended in mid-air, unaware, before suddenly dropping — the classic cartoon
delayed-gravity gag. Comedic timing, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** instant fall, no hang time, no comedic pause, realistic gravity.

**Prompt 22 — Feather-Light Fall:**
```
A [person] falls but descends with impossible lightness, drifting down like a
feather, swaying side to side, hair and clothing floating, taking a long time
to land. Dreamlike, soft light, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** fast fall, heavy impact, no drift, stiff body, normal gravity.

**Prompt 23 — Heavy Impact Crater:**
```
A [small object] falls and, impossibly, creates a huge [crater / shockwave /
explosion of debris] far beyond its size, the ground cratering dramatically.
Cinematic, over-the-top, slow-motion impact, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** realistic small impact, no crater, no debris, normal physics.

### 1.7 — MAGNETIC / INVISIBLE FORCE

**Mechanism:** An unseen force moves objects or people — pull, push, or telekinesis.

**Prompt 24 — Telekinesis Pull:**
```
A [person] extends a hand, and [an object across the room] slides across the
surface and flies into their palm, as if pulled by an invisible force. The
motion is smooth and continuous, the object spinning slightly. Dramatic
lighting, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** visible strings, hand touching object, object falling, no motion.

**Prompt 25 — Magnetic Attraction:**
```
Two [objects / people] are pulled toward each other by an invisible magnetic
force, sliding or leaning across the frame, unable to resist, until they nearly
touch. Surreal, smooth, physically lit, locked camera, 9:16 vertical.
```
**Negative:** no attraction, objects static, visible force, sudden jump.

## Negative-Prompt Cheat Sheet (Gravity Category)

Append to any gravity prompt to kill the most common failures:

```
frozen limbs, rigid pose, standing, walking normally, floating upright,
normal physics, ground contact, strings, wires, visible hands, visible support,
jumping instead of floating, extra limbs, distorted anatomy, wrong number of
fingers, blurry subject, jump cuts, broken motion, hard cuts, camera shake,
text, watermark, logo, play button
```

## When To Use Each Sub-Technique

| Sub-Technique | Emotional Vibe | Best For |
|---------------|---------------|----------|
| The Float | Serene, weightless, awe | Wellness, spirituality, "calm" hooks |
| The Fall | Urgent, kinetic, raw | High-energy openers, emotional releases |
| Reverse Gravity | Whimsical, magical, "wait what" | Food, beauty, playful brands |
| Zero-G | Dreamlike, surreal, wonder | Lifestyle, tech, "escape" content |
| Ground Opens | Shock, destabilizing, dramatic | Stakes, conflict, "everything changed" |
| Bounce/Impact | Comedic, exaggerated, fun | Humor, kids, entertainment |
| Magnetic Force | Powerful, controlling, mysterious | Tech, "influence" themes, magic |

## Teaching Tip (For Your Product)

When you mentor someone, don't just hand them the 25 prompts — teach them the
**"verb + direction + lock"** formula:

1. **Verb** = what the subject does (floats, falls, rises, sinks, bounces).
2. **Direction** = where it goes (up, down, sideways, through the frame).
3. **Lock** = "locked camera, no zoom/pan/tilt."

Anyone who internalizes that formula can generate an infinite number of
gravity-break prompts without ever memorizing a list. That's the skill you sell.
